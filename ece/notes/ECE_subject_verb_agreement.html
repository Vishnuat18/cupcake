Subject-Verb Agreement & Word Types

Unit II: Grammar and Sentence Structure (Study Notes)

1. Subject-Verb Agreement

Subject-verb agreement means that the subject and verb in a sentence must agree in number (singular/plural) and person (first/second/third).

With 'or / nor / either...or / neither...nor', the verb always agrees with the subject nearer to it — this is one of the most frequently tested rules in exams.

Important Rules

Singular & Plural Match: A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.
Example: He plays (singular) | They play (plural)
And rule: Two subjects joined by 'and' usually take a plural verb.
Example: Ram and Shyam are friends.
Or/Nor nearer rule: Subjects joined by 'or/nor/either...or/neither...nor' agree with the nearer subject.
Example: Either the boys or Ravi is at fault. (verb agrees with singular 'Ravi')
Collective Nouns: Collective nouns (team, family, class, jury) take a singular verb when acting as a single unit.
Example: The team is playing well.
Indefinite Pronouns: Pronouns like each, everyone, nobody, someone take a singular verb.
Example: Everyone has submitted the assignment.

Examples Table

Incorrect Correct
Each of the students have a book. Each of the students has a book.
The team are playing well. The team is playing well.

2. Nouns and Adverbs

i. Nouns

A noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, or idea. Nouns can be classified as:

  • Common noun – general name (e.g., boy, city).
  • Proper noun – specific name (e.g., Ravi, Chennai).
  • Collective noun – group acting as a unit (e.g., team, family, jury).
  • Abstract noun – idea or quality that cannot be touched (e.g., honesty, happiness).
  • Countable / Uncountable noun – items that can or cannot be counted (e.g., book/books vs. water, sugar, information).

ii. Adverbs

An adverb modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb, and usually answers how, when, where, or to what extent.

  • Adverb of manner – how an action is performed (e.g., slowly, carefully, beautifully).
  • Adverb of time – when it happens (e.g., now, yesterday, soon).
  • Adverb of place – where it happens (e.g., here, there, everywhere).
  • Adverb of frequency – how often it happens (e.g., always, never, often).
Shortcut – The 4-Question Scan: For any sentence, quickly scan:
1. Is there a preposition showing place, time, or direction?
2. Is the noun common, proper, collective, or abstract?
3. Does an adverb describe how, when, where, or how often?
4. Does the verb agree with the subject in number and person?

S-V Agreement & Grammar – Exercise

Part A – Fill in the Blanks (S-V Agreement)

Provide the correct verb form from the brackets.

  1. 'Neither of the boys ______________ present.' (was/were)
  2. 'The news ______________ shocking.' (is/are)
  3. 'Everybody ______________ happy today.' (is/are)
  4. 'My brother and my sister ______________ coming.' (is/are)

Answers:

  1. was ('Neither' takes a singular verb)
  2. is ('News' is singular/uncountable)
  3. is ('Everybody' takes a singular verb)
  4. are (Two subjects joined by 'and')

Part B – Choose the Best Option

  1. Choose the correct verb: 'Each of the students _______ a book.'
    a) have b) has c) having d) had have
  2. Choose the correct verb: 'Either the manager or the staff _______ responsible.'
    a) is b) are c) be d) being
  3. Choose the correct verb: 'The team _______ playing well.'
    a) is b) are c) were d) have

Answers:

  1. b) has ('Each' requires a singular verb)
  2. b) are ('staff' is plural and is closer to the verb)
  3. a) is (collective noun acting as one unit)

Part C – Correct the Sentence

Identify the error and provide the grammatically correct sentence.

  1. 'Everybody are happy today.'
  2. 'The jury have given its verdict.' (explain the correct version)
  3. 'Neither of the answers were correct.'

Answers:

  1. Everybody is happy today.
  2. The jury has given its verdict.
    (Reason: The collective noun 'jury' acts as a single unit here, as shown by singular pronoun 'its', so it takes the singular verb 'has'.)
  3. Neither of the answers was correct.
