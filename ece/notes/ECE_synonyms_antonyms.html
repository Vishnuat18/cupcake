Synonyms & Antonyms

Unit I: Vocabulary and Verbal Ability (Study Notes)

An antonym is a word that means the opposite of another word. Antonyms are important for improving vocabulary, comprehension, and for competitive examinations.

Recognising the antonym 'type' helps you answer faster in exams — graded antonyms allow a middle state, while complementary antonyms are strictly either/or.

Types of Antonyms

1. Graded Antonyms

These antonyms lie on a continuous scale, allowing for a middle ground or intermediate states between them.

Example: hot – cold (with warm, cool, tepid in between).
2. Complementary Antonyms

These antonyms are strictly mutually exclusive, meaning there is no middle ground or third option; if one is true, the other must be false.

Example: true – false, alive – dead.
3. Relational Antonyms

These antonyms show a relationship or opposite roles from different perspectives; one word cannot exist without the other.

Example: teacher – student, buy – sell, husband – wife.
Shortcut: Ask 'Is there anything in between?'
- If yes (e.g., hot → warm → cold), it is a graded antonym.
- If no (e.g., alive vs. dead), it is complementary.
- If the two words depend on each other (e.g., teacher & student), it is relational.

Antonyms – Exercise Questions

Part A – Fill in the Blanks

Provide the correct antonym in the blanks.

  1. The antonym of 'Ancient' is _______.
  2. The antonym of 'Brave' is _______.
  3. The antonym of 'Victory' is _______.
  4. 'Hot – Cold' is an example of a _______ antonym (graded/complementary).

Answers:

  1. Modern
  2. Cowardly
  3. Defeat
  4. graded

Part B – Choose the Best Answer

Select the correct option.

  1. Choose the correct antonym of 'Generous':
    a) Kind b) Stingy c) Rich d) Helpful
  2. Choose the correct antonym of 'Genuine':
    a) Real b) Original c) Fake d) True
  3. 'Alive – Dead' is an example of:
    a) Graded antonym b) Complementary antonym c) Relational antonym d) Synonym pair

Answers:

  1. b) Stingy
  2. c) Fake
  3. b) Complementary antonym

Part C – Short Answer

Explain your reasoning in your answer.

  1. State whether 'teacher–student' is a graded, complementary, or relational antonym pair.
  2. Give one example each of a graded and a complementary antonym pair.
  3. Why is 'true–false' considered a complementary antonym and not a graded one?

Answers:

  1. Relational antonym: they represent opposite roles that define one another.
  2. Graded: hot – cold (middle states like warm exist).
    Complementary: on – off (no middle states).
  3. Because there is no middle ground: a statement is either true or false. It cannot be partially true and partially false at the same time in standard logic.
