Vocabulary

Unit I: Vocabulary and Verbal Ability (Study Notes)

Vocabulary refers to the stock of words a person knows and uses in a language. A strong vocabulary helps in clear communication, better comprehension, and effective writing and speaking.

Words are best remembered when they are learned in groups (word families) rather than in isolation. For example, learning 'act', 'react', 'action', 'active' together builds a stronger mental link than learning each word separately.

Ways to Build Vocabulary

  • Reading newspapers, novels and journals regularly.
  • Learning synonyms and antonyms of new words.
  • Understanding root words, prefixes and suffixes.
  • Using a dictionary/thesaurus to check meaning and usage.
  • Using new words in sentences to remember them.
Shortcut: To guess the meaning of an unfamiliar word quickly, break it into Prefix + Root + Suffix.
Example: Unbelievable = Un- (not) + believe (root) + -able (capable of) = 'not capable of being believed'.

Key Terms

Term Meaning / Example
Synonym A word with the same or similar meaning (e.g., happy – glad).
Antonym A word with the opposite meaning (e.g., happy – sad).
Homonym Words that sound alike but differ in meaning/spelling (e.g., bear – bare).
Root word The base word to which prefixes or suffixes are added (e.g., 'act' in react, action).

One-Word Substitution

One-word substitution means replacing a group of words or a phrase with a single word that conveys the same meaning. This tests precision in vocabulary usage.

Many one-word substitutes follow recognisable patterns:

  • -phile means 'one who loves' (e.g., bibliophile).
  • -ist means 'one who studies or practises' (e.g., ornithologist).
  • a- at the start often means 'without' (e.g., atheist = without belief in God).
Shortcut: Learn substitutes in themed groups — places (library, sanctuary, orphanage), people (bibliophile, ornithologist, misanthrope), and 'one who cannot/does not' phrases (illiterate, atheist, teetotaller). Grouping by pattern makes recall faster in exams.

Common One-Word Substitutes

Phrase / Definition One Word
A person who loves books Bibliophile
A place where books are kept Library
One who cannot read or write Illiterate
A person who studies birds Ornithologist
One who does not believe in God Atheist
A short walk for pleasure Stroll

Advanced Vocabulary

In competitive exams, synonym/antonym questions are usually answered fastest by first eliminating options that mean something close to the opposite of what is required, rather than trying to define the given word in full.

Shortcut: When unsure of a word's exact meaning, judge its 'tone' — is it a generally positive word (e.g., abundant, optimistic) or negative (e.g., scarce, pessimistic)? Match the tone of the correct option to the tone of the question word.

Key Word Profiles

Word Meaning / Synonym Antonym
Abundant Existing or available in large quantities; overflowing (Plenty). Scarce
Optimistic Hopeful and confident about the future (Hopeful). Pessimistic
Frugal Sparing or economical as regards money or food (Careful with money / Economical). Extravagant / Lavish

Vocabulary – Exercise Questions

Part A – Fill in the Blanks

Provide the correct answer in the blanks.

  1. The word 'un_____' means 'not happy'. (happy)
  2. A word that means the same as another is called a ______.
  3. 'Dis' in 'disagree' is an example of a ______.
  4. The opposite of 'genuine' is ______.

Answers:

  1. unhappy
  2. synonym
  3. prefix
  4. fake (or counterfeit)

Part B – Choose the Best Answer

Select the correct option.

  1. Choose the synonym of 'Intelligent':
    a) Dull b) Clever c) Slow d) Weak
  2. Choose the antonym of 'Beautiful':
    a) Pretty b) Ugly c) Lovely d) Charming
  3. Which word is a homonym pair?
    a) big – large b) bear – bare c) hot – cold d) run – walk

Answers:

  1. b) Clever
  2. b) Ugly
  3. b) bear – bare

Part C – Match the Following

Match the words in Column A with their meaning in Column B.

Column A
  1. Diligent
  2. Benevolent
  3. Candid
  4. Robust
Column B
  1. Strong and healthy
  2. Honest and frank
  3. Hardworking
  4. Kind and generous

Answers:

  1. 1 – c (Hardworking)
  2. 2 – d (Kind and generous)
  3. 3 – b (Honest and frank)
  4. 4 – a (Strong and healthy)

Part D – One-Word Substitution Blanks

Provide the correct one-word substitute.

  1. A person who travels from place to place is called a _______.
  2. One who is easily deceived is called _______.
  3. A place where animals are kept for public viewing is a _______.
  4. A person who studies birds is called an _______.

Answers:

  1. wanderer / traveller / itinerant
  2. gullible
  3. zoo / menagerie
  4. ornithologist

Part E – One-Word Substitution Choices

Select the correct one-word substitute.

  1. Choose the correct one-word substitute for 'a person who loves books':
    a) Librarian b) Bibliophile c) Author d) Editor
  2. Choose the correct one-word substitute for 'one who does not believe in God':
    a) Theist b) Atheist c) Optimist d) Pessimist
  3. Choose the correct one-word substitute for 'a short walk for pleasure':
    a) Sprint b) Stroll c) Marathon d) Jog

Answers:

  1. b) Bibliophile
  2. b) Atheist
  3. b) Stroll

Part F – Give the One-Word Substitute

Write the single word that matches the phrase.

  1. A person who cannot read or write.
  2. A place where books are kept.
  3. One who leaves one's country to settle in another.

Answers:

  1. Illiterate
  2. Library
  3. Emigrant

Part G – Advanced Vocabulary Exercises

1. Fill in the Blanks

  1. The synonym of 'Abundant' is _______.
  2. The antonym of 'Optimistic' is _______.
  3. 'Frugal' means a person who is _______ (careful/wasteful) with money.
  4. The synonym of 'Frugal' is _______ (economical/lavish).

2. Choose the Best Answer

  1. Find the synonym of 'Abundant':
    a) Scarce b) Plenty c) Little d) Rare
  2. Find the antonym of 'Optimistic':
    a) Hopeful b) Pessimistic c) Cheerful d) Confident
  3. Give the meaning of the word 'Frugal':
    a) Extravagant b) Careful with money c) Generous d) Wealthy

3. Short Answer Questions

  1. Use the word 'meticulous' in a sentence of your own.
  2. Give one synonym and one antonym for the word 'Robust'.
  3. Explain, in one line, the difference between 'frugal' and 'stingy'.

Answers:

1. Blanks:
  • plenty / profuse
  • pessimistic
  • careful
  • economical
2. Choices:
  • b) Plenty
  • b) Pessimistic
  • b) Careful with money
3. Short Answer:
  • Example sentence: He was meticulous in checking the code for errors.
  • Robust: Synonym = strong / healthy | Antonym = weak / fragile.
  • Difference: 'Frugal' means being careful and sensible with money (economical) without wasting, while 'stingy' means being meanly reluctant to spend money even when it is necessary.
